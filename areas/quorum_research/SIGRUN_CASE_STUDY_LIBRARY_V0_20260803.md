## §4 · Experimental practice

**Standard form for every studio experiment:**

```
HYPOTHESIS   <population> will <action> when given <treatment>
POPULATION   named market, n, time window
TREATMENT    the one variable that changes
SUCCESS      external measurement + threshold
FALSIFIER    the observation that makes the hypothesis FALSE (not "retry")
POWER        n needed before the null is informative
DECISION_BY  UTC timestamp
CONFOUNDS    named and controlled
```

**Power, concretely.** For a landing page at a plausible 2% cold conversion,
**n≈50 visits distinguishes nothing** — zero conversions at n=50 is fully
consistent with a 2% true rate (p≈0.36). ⭐ **You need n≈150 for a zero-result to
be meaningfully surprising, and n≈300 to separate 1% from 3%.** For cold email at
a 5–10% reply rate, **n=40 gives 87–98% chance of ≥1 reply if the offer works —
so zero replies at n=40 IS informative.** ⭐ **That is why the 150 staged drafts
are a genuinely powered experiment and a landing page alone is not.**

**Confounds to control:** day-of-week (send Tue–Thu), seasonality (August is slow
for B2B), AI-hype saturation (every inbox has AI pitches), and — the one that
bit us — **channel death** (Upwork RSS returned HTTP 410 Gone on all 7 feeds).

### ⛔ Can we run "hundreds of experiments daily"? No — and the reason matters.

⭐ **Split trials into two kinds.** **Internal trials** (variant generation,
grading, held-out probes) are compute-bound: **hundreds per day is realistic and
Codex already does it.** **External trials** (a real stranger seeing a real offer)
are audience-bound, and the ceiling is brutal: ~1 self-post per subreddit per day
before moderation, cold-email volume caps on a warmed domain, portal reviews in
**1–3 weeks**, HN front-page attempts effectively once per artifact.

⭐ **Realistic throughput: hundreds of internal trials/day feeding 3–5 EXTERNAL
experiments per WEEK.** And per row 115, only external ones count. **The
bottleneck is not compute and never was — it is the number of strangers you can
put an offer in front of, and your approval rate as the champion selector.**

## §6 · Top-5 failures to avoid

1. ⭐ **Publishing instead of distributing.** ClearNoteLab (0 signups) and our own
   0-star repo. **A launch is an act of distribution, not an act of upload.**
2. ⭐ **Copying a warm-audience playbook while cold.** Levels/Lou/Postma/Dinh all
   had audiences. **Copy AppAlchemy (Reddit, $0, no pre-audience) instead.**
3. **Mistaking first-$ for durable-$.** $837 MRR → $0 in 3 months. Churn kills
   after the win, and D2 notes AI-app retention is *worse* (21.1% vs 30.7%).
4. **Solution before problem.** The most-cited pattern: elegant tools for
   unpainful problems.
5. ⭐ **Underpowered nulls.** Killing a lane on n=20 when n=20 could not have
   detected success. **Specify power before running, or you will retire good
   lanes and keep bad ones.**
