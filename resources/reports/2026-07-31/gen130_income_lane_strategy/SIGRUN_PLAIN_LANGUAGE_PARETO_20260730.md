## 4 · The four substrates, and why "unoptimized" is the wrong word

You have Claude, ChatGPT-Codex, ChatGPT-cloud, and the free-vendor mesh, and you
called them unoptimized. I think the diagnosis is sharper than that.

**They are not badly tuned. They are pointed inward.** Every one of them
currently loops on internal state — dashboards, chains, receipts, gates. Not one
of them has a job that ends with a message reaching a stranger. That is why the
system can run at full tilt and produce zero dollars. You do not have a
throughput problem, you have an aiming problem.

Here is what "optimized" means for each.

**Claude.** Optimized means Claude only does judgment: strategy, positioning,
adversarial review, spec-writing. Right now it is also burning frontier tokens
on silence-checks, carrier returns, and pheromone emits — work that needs no
model at all. Blocker: your Sonnet apex seat is vacant, so seven valkyries have
no local decision-maker and every choice routes through a bottleneck.

**ChatGPT-Codex.** Optimized means Codex owns all code-authoring and every
scheduled heartbeat, because it runs on your host with real file access and
without Claude's write-gate. It is the only lane that can actually land code.
Blocker: your dashboard says one of eight apexes is verified running. The rest
are asserted. Nobody has checked whether those hourly loops are emitting.

**ChatGPT-cloud.** Optimized means it does research and prospect enrichment and
carries the human-facing lanes, because it has the widest reach. Blocker: nobody
is actually there. The migrations are deferred and it is a plan, not a substrate.

**Free-vendor mesh.** Optimized means bulk cheap parallel work — enrichment,
mass drafting, cross-family review. Blocker: Surtr is stuck and has never
emitted a live receipt. But I want to correct something: the local Ollama trio
*does* work. Earlier today it returned five real reviews with genuine
disagreement between model families. That is a working adversarial-review
capability you already have and are not using. The vendor mesh is stuck; the
local mesh is alive.

**So the loop-engineering fix is one sentence:** give each substrate a job whose
success condition is external. Codex's heartbeat should fail if no message left
the building. Claude's session should end on a draft ready to send, not a
document. The mesh's job is to argue with those drafts before they go. Right now
every loop can score green while the bank balance does not move, and that is not
a tuning problem — it is the wrong success condition, wired in four places.
