## §7 · THE HIVE — WHICH PARTS ARE LOAD-BEARING

You asked for the swarm to be "productive like bees making honey." Tested
honestly, part of the analogy is doing real work and part is decoration that
imports a wrong idea. Separating them:

### LOAD-BEARING — keep, these constrain the design

**① Honey requires nectar from outside the hive.** This is the diagnosis of the
last 30 days and the strongest thing in the metaphor. 609 autonomous commits,
**zero artifacts a stranger could see** (measured, gen-130 §1). The hive built
beautiful comb and never flew out. **Comb is not honey. No forager leaves, no
honey, regardless of how good the comb is.** Every §6 move is a foraging trip.

**② Foraging economics: a forager returning less energy than it burns is a net
loss to the colony.** This is the metaphor's best gift because it converts to a
mechanical rule: **any loop costing more tokens/hours than the external effect
it produces should be switched off.** By that rule, 78-of-81 scheduled tasks
disabled is *correct*, and the 3 currently enabled — all heartbeat, no
production — should also be off until they carry nectar. The metaphor arrives
at the same verdict as the capacity audit, from a different direction. That
independent agreement is worth something.

**③ Stigmergy — bees coordinate through the comb, not through messages.** No
bee tells another bee what to do; they read and modify shared structure. HFO's
chain/blackboard is genuinely this, and it is the reason the write-path gate
(gen-130 L1/L2) is an *existential* bug rather than an inconvenience: **a hive
whose comb cannot be written to has no coordination mechanism at all.** The
metaphor correctly predicts that the write gate outranks every feature.

**④ Many cheap scouts, one expensive commitment.** Bees scout many sites in
parallel and commit once, collectively. Parallel valkyrie dispatch → quorum is
the same shape, and it is the swarm's actual advantage in the factory: many
cheap branded variants generated in parallel, one graded and shipped.

### DECORATIVE OR ACTIVELY MISLEADING — drop or repair

**⑤ Queen–drone–worker ↦ apex–lieutenant–valkyrie. DROP THIS.** It is wrong in
a way that will cost you. **Bee castes are reproductive roles, not a command
hierarchy. The queen does not direct anything** — she lays eggs; colony
decisions are made by workers, in aggregate, without her. Mapping authority
tiers onto bee castes imports a chain-of-command the actual insect does not
have, and it will quietly license top-down dispatch in a system whose real
strength (③, ④) is decentralized. **Keep the tiers; stop calling them bees.**

**⑥ Waggle dance ↦ quorum voting. HALF TRUE — and the other half is a real
design upgrade.** The waggle dance is not a vote; it is **advertising with a
staked cost**. A bee dances with vigour proportional to site quality, and her
dancing *decays* — she must be re-convinced by re-visiting. Nobody can advertise
indefinitely for free. **HFO's quorum votes are currently free to cast and do
not decay.** If you want this mapping to be load-bearing rather than
decorative, make votes cost something and expire. That is an actual
specification extracted from the analogy, and it is the only part of §7 that
generates new work.

**⑦ 1+8+64+512 as hive population. DECORATIVE.** A hive's population is
metabolically bounded; HFO's is token-budget bounded. The *existence* of a
budget constraint is real; the specific numbers come from your architecture,
not from bees. Do not defend them with the metaphor.

**⑧ PARA ↦ comb structure. DECORATIVE but harmless.** PARA is a filing
convention. Comb is a shared-state coordination medium — that role is already
played by the chain (③). Two different things wearing one name; keep them
separate.

### Refined analogy

> **HFO is a hive in its foraging economics and its stigmergy — not in its
> governance.** Use the metaphor to decide *what leaves the hive* and *what
> gets culled for burning more than it returns.* Do not use it to decide who

## §8.0 · THE CORRECTION THAT MAKES THE TOURNAMENT WORK

You said two things in one breath, and they are in direct tension:

> *"my attention is limited and I'm so busy doing CPR"* … *"each apex would take
> their own funding lane … we can see who makes the most money in a week"*

**Income requires world-effect. Every world-effect gate — send, publish, spend,
invoice, push — is operator-gated, by design, and correctly so.** So eight apex
racing to produce income all queue at *one* single-threaded resource: **you.**

**An 8-lane income tournament does not divide your attention burden. It
multiplies the demand on it by eight.** As literally specified, it makes the CPR
worse. That is the honest red-team and I am leading with it.

### The fix — change what they compete on

> ## ⭐ **STAMPED: apex do not compete on income. They compete for a fixed weekly budget of operator approval-clicks, and the fitness function is INCOME PER OPERATOR-CLICK CONSUMED.**

Mechanics:

- **You set a hard weekly budget of approval-clicks.** Recommend **10/week**
  (one click ≈ one send/publish/spend decision ≈ 2 minutes of you). That is
  ~20 minutes of operator attention per week, total, for the entire hive.
- **Apex compete by queueing *seed-approvable* work** — fully prepared,
  one-click-to-fire artifacts. Preparing costs zero operator attention.
- **The leaderboard is `$ ÷ clicks_consumed`, not `$`.**
- **An apex that queues 40 items, burns 8 clicks and returns $0 LOSES to an
  apex that queues 2 items, burns 1 click and returns $500.**

Why this inverts the problem correctly: under `$/click`, **an apex is penalized
for consuming your attention.** The tournament's selection pressure now points
*at* your constraint instead of against it. Eight agents optimizing to spend
less of you is exactly "loop engineering and agents helping me." Eight agents
optimizing gross income is eight agents competing to interrupt you.

## §8.4 · TOURNAMENT AND EVOLUTIONARY SELECTION

### Fitness

**Primary:** `$ ÷ operator-clicks-consumed` (§8.0).
**Tiebreak 1:** total `queued_approvable` (throughput).
**Tiebreak 2:** false-greens *received* from Huginn+Muninn, ascending
(reliability). An apex that wins on dollars while collecting vetoes has not won.

### Cadence

| checkpoint | period | action |
|---|---|---|
| **weekly** | Mon | Sigrún posts the leaderboard; **no selection action** — one week is noise at these volumes |
| **monthly** | 1st | lane *tuning* only: reprice, retarget, reallocate click budget between apex |
| **quarterly** | 2026-11-01 | **selection event** — retire/reassign lanes |
| yearly | 2027-08 | roster-level review, archetype diversity audit |

Weekly leaderboards with no selection attached is deliberate. At 1–4 income
events per *month*, weekly selection is fitting noise, and fitting noise is how
you get a monoculture that optimized a sampling artifact.

### Retirement protocol — reassign the LANE, not the APEX

> **The chain is the asset. The lane is the assignment.**

1. A losing lane is **retired**; the apex is **not deleted**.
2. The apex's chain, soul, and heritage stay durable and readable forever
   (SR-4: roster history is lineage history).
3. The lane transfers to a winning apex or is sunset with a dated overturn
   criterion.
4. The retired apex is **reassigned** to a niche the archive is thin in, not
   fired. Deleting an apex destroys lineage to fix an assignment — a category
   error and a permanent loss.
5. A retirement row is written to the apex's own chain by its lieutenant,
   including `honest_flaw`.

**Replacement (a genuinely new callsign) is reserved for one case only: no
substrate exists for the apex to run on.** Everything else is reassignment.

### Cross-apex learning

Apex publish **patterns**, not just results: a `pattern` row in their chain
naming a reusable move, its cost, and its outcome. Any apex may adopt one and
must credit the source in its own chain. Adoption is tracked — **an apex whose
patterns are widely adopted earns a diversity credit at the quarterly review**,
which prevents the tournament from punishing the apex that discovers something
everyone then copies. Without that credit, the discoverer's edge gets competed
away and discovery stops. This is the tournament's most likely silent failure
mode and the credit is the cheapest available fix.

### MAP-Elites diversity — keep the archive wide

Niche descriptor per lane (2-D behavioral space):

- **axis 1 — cycle length:** transactional (days) ↔ relational (quarters)
- **axis 2 — leverage source:** operator-hours ↔ artifact-reuse

| L-B Fenrir, factory | relational × **artifact-reuse** |

**Keep the elite of each occupied niche even if it earns less.** L-A and L-C

The gate has one distinction that decides whether it helps or hurts:

> **An explicit `NOT_FOUND` is a SUCCESSFUL query. Only an error or timeout

everything `proposed` not `wired`, and any historical claim prefixed

gate**; turning on S1 before the store is populated would andon every wake on

same cure.

*removed* — it is memory **never being loaded**, and the gap then being filled
fluently. That distinction is load-bearing: the cure for "loading never
happened" is a mandatory pre-action query; the cure for "memory was destroyed"
would be backups. This contract is the former.

## §10.4 · §7 REVISED — the hive has a negative signal, and HFO never built it
