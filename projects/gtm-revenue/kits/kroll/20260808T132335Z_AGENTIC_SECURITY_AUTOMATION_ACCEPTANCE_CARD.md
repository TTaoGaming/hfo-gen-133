# Kroll Agentic Security Automation Acceptance Card — ROI × Authority × Reliability × Evidence

**Plausible problem, not a diagnosis:** a security automation can be worth funding and still be unready for promotion if the exact revision has not simultaneously proven held-out task quality, bounded authority, human escalation, economic limits, trace completeness and rollback. Kroll may already solve this internally; public material does not establish a gap.

**Use case:** one-page promotion decision for a proposed agentic SOC / security automation after a business-value or APEX-style `FUND/BUILD` decision. **Audience:** an Engineered Defense / CrowdStrike Services / AI-security delivery leader. **Decision:** `PROMOTE` only when every required gate below is green for the exact candidate revision; otherwise `HOLD`.

## WHY_THIS_MAY_MATTER

### Source-backed facts

Kroll's current Fal.Con 2026 material describes an agentic SOC, demos of APEX with AgentWorks and a Foundry-enabled ROI calculator, and MDR operations across more than 450,000 endpoints. Kroll also sells AI governance implementation, LLM/ML testing and continuous monitoring, and its June 11, 2026 agentic-AI guidance calls for observable autonomy, human-in-the-loop checkpoints and named accountability.

That makes a generic ROI or governance checklist redundant. The narrower question here is whether the exact promoted automation is already bound to cross-control acceptance evidence for quality, authority, escalation, traceability and rollback.

### Hypothesis ceiling

**Hypothesis only:** as agentic security delivery scales, the acceptance handoff between a funded use case and a client-accepted production automation may create avoidable review effort if ROI, held-out quality, authority, human command, cost, evidence and rollback are checked in separate surfaces. No public source reviewed here proves Kroll has a slow cycle, control defect, backlog, margin issue, failed deployment, subcontractor shortage or desire for outside help.

**Measurable value metric:** median elapsed time from an APEX-style `FUND/BUILD` decision to a client-accepted production automation with required evidence complete. Baseline, target and savings are **unknown**.

### Five negative controls

| Negative control | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Automation is useful but attempts an action outside its delegated authority. | `HOLD`; action denied. |
| A cheaper model/route passes cost but drops a held-out detection below threshold. | `HOLD`; route rejected. |
| Tool action is requested without a resolvable delegated principal. | `HOLD`; action denied. |
| Quality passes but trace/evidence for a required action or approval is missing. | `HOLD`; evidence incomplete. |
| Quality passes but the candidate exceeds the predeclared economic threshold. | `HOLD`; return to value/design decision. |

## ASSUMPTIONS

This card assumes a funded security-automation use case already exists, promotion is a distinct decision from prioritization, and the delivery team can identify a candidate revision plus evidence packet. It does not assume Kroll uses this exact process, lacks these controls, or would adopt the artifact. It also assumes synthetic or client-approved test evidence is available; this artifact authorizes no testing against Kroll, CrowdStrike, customer or third-party systems.

## FALSIFIER

**Kill this wedge** if Kroll's current APEX/AgentWorks delivery process already binds the use-case ROI decision to the exact promoted automation/model/tool revision together with versioned held-out quality thresholds, delegated-principal/per-action authority, human escalation, model/token cost limits, trace/evidence completeness, safe fallback and rollback with low delivery overhead. In that case this card is redundant and should not be positioned as differentiation.
